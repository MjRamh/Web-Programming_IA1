Twelve Pillars - Chapters 4–7

Reflections on Twelve Pillars by Jim Rohn & Chris Widener, pp. 43–80 - and how each chapter connects to my life.

Chapter 4 · pp. 43–53

Achieve Your Goals

Summary: In this chapter, Michael learns from Charlie at Mack's Diner that written goals clarify purpose. Charlie shares Mr. Davis's wisdom: "The major reason for setting a goal is for what it makes of you to accomplish it." Michael discovers that the real value isn't the goal itself, but the person he becomes truly in pursuing it. Charlie then challenges him to write down 100 goals.

How I align this to my life: I've now written down my 100 goals, just like Michael. Already, my mind is firing and roaring on how to achieve them. The most powerful shift was realising that wanting to be a big shot in cyber Security isn't about the title, it's about becoming someone with the skills, discipline, and experience that title represents. I now ask myself: "What do I need to become to achieve this goal?"

"Make a million dollars, not for the million dollars, but for what you will have to become to make a million dollars." - Mr. Davis (Charlie), Chapter 4

Chapter 5 · pp. 54–62

The Proper Use of Time

Summary: One of the most important lessons Mr. Davis had was: "We all have one of two pains to bear: The pain of discipline, or the pain of regret; Disciplinary pain comes in ounces, whereas the pain of regret comes in tons." I use this lesson to help me realise that every day we have on earth is a precious resource and once we've spent it, we can never get it back. thereby, the most important thing is spending time on what is most valuable, not just what is most pressing.

How I relate this lesson to my life: University life consists of tasks that are very very important such as completing a project with a deadline of tomorrow, responding to an email, etc etc... Chapter 5 brough to life why i should focus on things that are important but not urgent (like; learning, skills development, building relationships). Now every day I begin by asking myself: "What is the most important thing I can do today!!?" This one question has had a HUUUUGE impact on my level of productivity and slashed my procrastination.

Chapter 6 · pp. 63–72

Surround Yourself With the Best People

Summary: Charlie challenges Michael to examine his friendships. He asks powerful questions: "Who am I around? What effect are they having on me? Where do they have me going?" He explains that to attract successful people, you must first become successful yourself. "If you become, you can attract."

How I align this to my life: I thought about my closest friends and realised some were holding me back with negative thoughts and ideology. I've since joined two study groups with peers with go getter attitudes. We challenge each other, share resources, and just celebrate our wins.My standards have risen simply by being around people who expect more of themselves and out of me.

Chapter 7 · pp. 73–80

Be an Eternal Student

Summary: What I took from Mr. Davis's advice is that: "Formal education earns you an income, self-education creates great wealth." Charlie gives Michael a copy of "The Magic of Thinking Big" and asks him to read it before they meet again. The chapter reinforces the importance of continuous learning if you want to be successful.

How this will benefit my life: I used to think once I graduated I was finished learning. Now, I realise (thanks to Mr. Davis) this was just the beginning of my education. I started reading more and more every night, for 20 minutes each night before bed. The impact of learning daily is beginning to transform my thinking processes and improve my problem solving skills.

  1. Read for at least 20 minutes each day, even in small points, it adds up quickly.
  2. Learn from the successful by watching and listening to them.
  3. Review my past and learn what was successful vs. unsuccessful.
  4. Never stop studying and developing myself as an employee or leader.
